The region has been inhabited since ancient times by the  Gaetuli  , who, far from Roman and Byzantine influence, led a nomadic lifestyle and traveled across a vast territory stretching from Mauritania to Tunisia. These warrior peoples carried out raids and frequently came into conflict with the Romanized Berbers of Tell and the Roman or Byzantine authorities.

The Medracen Numidian mausoleum dating from the 3rd century.

The history of the Titteri region is therefore intertwined with that of the central Maghreb, marked by successive invasions by the Romans from the 2nd to the 5th century.
The Vandals also left their mark without being able to establish their colonization.
The Byzantines , in turn, encountered resistance from the indigenous tribes.
Numidians , Moors and Getulians became the Zenetes thanks to the mixing that took place between the different tribes at that time.

After the Islamization of the central Maghreb, this region became the territory of the  Zenata . The region then came under the domination of the  Rostemides  (707-909).

The Rostemid Dynasty

Until the 7th century, the Central Maghreb, called Zenitia by Ibn Khaldoun , remained independent.
After the Islamization of the Central Maghreb, this region became the territory of the Zenetes .
The region then came under the domination of the Rostemides (707-909).
Between the 7th and 9th centuries, the Arab conquest, which, favored by the Kharedjites of Tahert , extended the sovereignty of the Rostemides over all the high plains. Towards the middle of the 11th century, the invasion of the Hillalian
Bedouins driven out of Egypt by the Fatimids , had serious repercussions on the stability of the Berber kingdoms, putting an end to the Hamadite dynasty . Thanks to the invasion of the Beni Hilall , the nomadic Zenetes tribes drove out the sedentary Sanhadja .

Ottoman period

During the Ottoman era, the  Beylik  of  Titteri  (1546-1834), whose capital was  Medea  , extended its domination over this entire region of high plains.

The provinces of the Regency of Algiers

From the 14th to the 19th century, there was Turkish domination and the conquest of Medea by Arroudj .
The Beylik of Titteri (1546-1834), whose capital was Medea, extended his domination over the entire region of the high plains.
In 1517, Baba Arroudj occupied Medea and installed a garrison of Turkish infantry and some Andalusian horsemen who had emigrated from Spain.
The first Bey appointed to head this Beylik was Redjeb , Bey from 1547 to 1633.
The number of Beys who governed Titteri for 227 years from 1548 to 1775 was 18.
Bey Sofra, who headed the Beylik since 1772, died during the expedition he led against the Tribes of Ouleds Nail who, for several years, did not pay their taxes.
The successive failures of the Turkish troops allowed the establishment in Médéa of the permanent seat of the Beylik of Titteri to better assert the shaky authority of the pashas.
The choice of the divan fell on Mustapha El Ouaznadji Ben Souleimain who was named Bey of Titteri which he administered for nineteen years from 1775 to 1794.
Mohamed Frira , nicknamed the throat cutter, administered Titteri from 1794 to 1799 and was killed during an expedition.
His successor Ibrahim Tlemçani retained power until 1801.
Bey Hassan occupied the governorate of Titteri and made several sorties against the Ouled Nail .
He had the mosque called Djamaa El Ahmer built in Médéa near the gate of the gardens. Mohamed Bey succeeded him until 1809 and was in turn strangled by order of the new Pasha . Hadj Ali Boumezrag closes the list of Beys of Titteri that he administered from 1819 to 1830. He died a few years later in Alexandria in Egypt where he lived his exile.

The beginnings of the colonial conquest
From 1830 to 1847, it was the French conquest of Titteri whose columns met with stiff resistance led by Emir Abdelkader .
Médéa did not fall until 1837 while the struggle continued in the south and the Ouled Nail .
The Smala was taken by surprise in the absence of the Emir .
Throughout the French period Taguine remained an important waiting area for transhumance and a modest center where a Caïd dependent on the mixed commune of Chellala resided .
It was never a center of European settlement .

Origin and evolution of the name

The commune of  Z’malet El Émir Abdelkader , located in the  wilaya of Tiaret , was formerly known as  Taguine . It was renamed in homage to  Emir Abdelkader , an emblematic figure of the Algerian resistance against French colonization in the 19th century.

The word  “Z’malet”  refers to the  zmalas , which were traveling camps that brought together the families, fighters and property of Emir Abdelkader during his fight against the French army.

Organization of the family

The Smala of Emir Abdelkader

  • Emir Abd el-Kader  always organized the smala according to the same principle: it consisted of four circular and concentric enclosures where each douar, each family, each individual had their fixed and marked place, according to their rank, their usefulness, their functions, or the confidence they inspired. When the smala arrived at its shelter, the emir’s tent was erected in the center of the ground that the camp was to cover.
  • It was immediately surrounded by the tents of the intimate servants and the principal relatives of Abd el-Kader  who composed the first enclosure:  5 douars
  • The second included the douars of  Khalifa Ben Allal  and his relatives, those of the regular infantry and some important chiefs:  10 douars .
  • The third was formed by the  Hachem-Cherraga  and the  Hachem-Gharaba  :  207 douars .
  • The fourth enclosure, more or less close to the main enclosures, depending on the difficulties of the terrain, the water, the woods or the pastures, was formed by seven nomadic tribes who served as guides and protection for the smala in the desert:  146 douars .
  • A total of  368 douars , each with fifteen to twenty tents. The population of this itinerant city can be estimated at twenty thousand souls, and   the number of combatants armed with rifles  at five thousand , including five hundred  regular infantrymen and  two thousand cavalrymen .

The capture of Abdelkader’s Smala (1843): a major event

The most significant event in the history of the region is  the capture of the Smala of Abdelkader  by French forces on  May 16, 1843 .

Historical context

  • Since  1832 , Emir Abdelkader had been waging a  war of resistance  against the French occupation.
  • To escape enemy attacks, he had organized a  smala , an immense nomadic community of several thousand people:
    • Its fighters and their families.
    • Craftsmen, scholars and religious people.
    • Livestock, wealth and military resources
    • In May 1843, the Smala was established in the region of  Taguine  (current  Z’malet El Émir Abdelkader ).

The attack on the Duke of Aumale

  • The smala had spent the end of the winter of 1843 two days’ march south of  Tagdempt . Informed that they were being pursued, they wandered for some time and found themselves on May 16 at the source of  Taguine . Governor-General  Bugeaud  had been informed of the presence of the smala in the vicinity of  Boghari  (currently  Ksar El Boukhari , Wilaya of  Médéa ), but the location was unknown.
  • He ordered General  Lamoricière and the Duke  of Aumale  to pursue him. The prince left  Boghari  with 1,300 infantrymen and 600 horses. Three days later, he learned that the smala was 80 kilometers south of  Goudjila (currently  Nadorah , Wilaya of  Tiaret ). To reach it, it was necessary to cross twenty leagues in one go without a drop of water. While the soldiers were looking for the  Taguine spring  to quench their thirst, the bachagha  Ahmar ben Ferhat  (bachagha of the  ouled Ayad ,  Theniet El Had ) came to inform the prince of the unexpected presence of the smala at this same spring.
  • 300 of the Emir ‘s companions were killed during the assault and many were taken prisoner, considering the number he kept only 3,000, and let all the others go.
  • Among the prisoners were the entire family of Mohamed Ben Allal Ben Embarek , and that of El Kharoubi , first secretary of the Emir , and those of several other influential leaders.
  • On the 17th, the Duke of Aumale stayed at Taguine to give the column a little rest.
  • The tents and booty that could not be transported were burned.
  • The prince then fell back on Boghari and from there on Médéa , from where his prize was taken to Algiers .

Geographic location of Z’malet El Émir Abdelkader

Z’malet El Émir Abdelkader is a commune located in the Tiaret province, in northwestern Algeria. It is approximately 250 km southwest of Algiers. The commune reaches an altitude of 850 meters and is located 156 km southeast of Tiaret, the capital of the province.

Z’malet El Émir Abdelkader is located in the valley of the Touil wadi, which only flows during rainy weather, and only its major floods reach the confluence with the Ouerk wadi and the Nahr Ouassel near Chahbounia, 75 km further north.

Oued Touil

Fire brigade response to the May 2025 flood

Geographic coordinates:

  • Latitude: 35.6686° N
  • Longitude: 1.1478° E

Relief and climate:

  • The region is characterized by a relief of high plateaus, with hills and plains suitable for agriculture and livestock farming.
  • The climate is semi-arid, with hot, dry summers and cold winters.

Z’malet El Emir Abdelkader is located in the heart of the alfatière steppe, but in a location favored by the abundance of springs.
A few wetlands can remain in winter near the banks of the wadi, which has advantages for the sheep: plant growth, and disadvantages: the spread of parasites.
The soils are adequate despite a slight salinity due to Triassic deep water upwelling along a short fault running along the heights near the Chebka hills.
Since Z’malet El Emir Abdelkader is at an altitude of 850 meters, it is cold in winter, and sometimes a colder-than-usual winter decimates the flocks.
Sheep from the Saharan Atlas and Laghouat passed through Z’malet El Emir Abdelkader in spring and late summer.

Communication axes:

  • Main roads: The commune is accessible by roads connecting Tiaret, Djelfa, Ksar Chellala and other surrounding towns.
  • Transport: Mainly road, connecting rural areas to nearby urban centres.

Z’malet El Émir Abdelkader is therefore a strategic commune from a historical and agricultural perspective, benefiting from a location in the heart of the Algerian High Plateaux.

]]> Population & Culture /en/population-culture-2/ Sun, 09 Mar 2025 14:06:53 +0000 http://taguinezea.local/?p=72 Population and culture of Z’malet El Émir Abdelkader

  1. Population
    The commune of Z’malet El Émir Abdelkader, located in the wilaya of Tiaret, has a relatively modest population, composed mainly of rural families living from agriculture and livestock.
  • Number of inhabitants: The population is estimated at a few thousand inhabitants.
  • Density: Low, due to the rural and semi-arid nature of the region.
  • Way of life: Mainly agricultural, with a strong preservation of local traditions.
    Tribal community: The population comes from several Arab and Berber tribes that historically occupied the region, some of which played a key role in the resistance against French colonization.
  1. Culture and traditions
    A. Legacy of Emir Abdelkader
    Since the city is named in honor of Emir Abdelkader, his memory is very present in the local culture. There are historical accounts, poems, and events commemorating his fight against the French occupation.
  • B. Language and Religion
  • Main language: Algerian dialectal Arabic is the most commonly spoken, with Berber influence in some expressions.
  • Official language: Classical Arabic is used in administration and education.
  • Religion: Islam is at the heart of social and spiritual life, with religious practices deeply rooted in daily life.


C. Feasts and Celebrations
Religious festivals:

Eid El-Fitr and Eid El-Adha are widely celebrated, with family gatherings and sheep sacrifices.
Mawlid (birth of the Prophet Muhammad صلّى اللّه عليه و سلّم) is an occasion for prayers and festivities.
Celebrations:

  • July 5 Independence Day is a national holiday in Algeria commemorating the independence of colonial Algeria from France on July 5, 1962. Celebrated every year on July 5, the holiday is marked by the organization of ceremonies glorifying the history of the Algerian people’s struggle. These include parades, marches, concerts, and fireworks.
  • November 1 The Declaration of November 1, 1954, also known as the FLN Manifesto, is the first appeal addressed by the National Liberation Front (FLN) to the Algerian people, in connection with the “Red All Saints’ Day” day of action, marking the beginning of the Algerian War of Independence.
  • Yennayer is New Year’s Day in the agrarian calendar used since ancient times by the Berbers across North Africa. Celebrated depending on the region from January 12 to 14 each year, it corresponds to the first day of January in the Julian calendar, which is 13 days behind the Gregorian calendar, and begins on the 14th of each year.

Traditional weddings:

Ceremonies with music, folk songs and wearing traditional clothing.
Use of henna for brides.


D. Music and folklore
The traditional music of the region is influenced by chaâbi and malhoun, poetic and musical styles popular in Algeria.
Bedouin songs and folk dances are often present during local festivals.
E. Local gastronomy


The local cuisine is simple but tasty, based on agricultural products and livestock:

  • Mutton couscous (an essential dish for special occasions).
  • Rechta (traditional Algerian pasta).
  • Chakhchoukha (dish made from crumbled pancake and spicy sauce).
  • Fermented milk (L’ben) and dates as an accompaniment.
  1. Social life and solidarity
    Solidarity is a key element of the community, especially in times of crisis or celebration.
    Elders play an important role in the transmission of knowledge and cultural values.
    The souk (market) is a place for meetings and commercial but also social exchanges.

Conclusion
Z’malet El Émir Abdelkader is a commune where traditions remain deeply rooted. Its population, although rural and dispersed, retains a strong attachment to its history, particularly through the memory of Emir Abdelkader. The local culture is marked by religious celebrations, authentic gastronomy and a way of life based on mutual aid and the transmission of ancestral values.

Economy of Z’malet El Émir Abdelkader
The economy of Z’malet El Émir Abdelkader is mainly based on agriculture, livestock farming and local trade. Like many municipalities in the wilaya of Tiaret, it benefits from a semi-arid climate suitable for certain crops and sheep farming.

  1. Agriculture
    Agriculture is the main activity in the region, favored by the presence of vast agricultural lands. The main crops are:
  • Cereals: durum wheat, soft wheat, barley (adapted to the semi-arid climate).
  • Legumes: lentils, chickpeas.
  • Some market gardening on small farms.
    Agriculture is mainly based on rainfall, although some areas benefit from irrigation.
  1. Livestock
    Z’malet El Émir Abdelkader is known for its sheep and cattle breeding, which plays an important economic role:
  • Sheep: production of wool and meat, essential for festivities (Eid, weddings).
  • Cattle: production of milk and meat.
  • Goats: breeding in smaller quantities.
    This breeding supplies regional and national markets, making the municipality an important player in the agri-food sector.
  1. Crafts and local trade
    Crafts and trade remain secondary but essential activities:
  • Leatherwork and traditional carpets made locally.
  • Small businesses: grocery stores, weekly market with sale of local products.
  • Sale of livestock and agricultural products on regional markets.

Exhibition of arts and crafts on the occasion of July 5, 2011

  1. Infrastructure and economic development
    Presence of agricultural cooperatives helping farmers.
    Rural development programs to encourage irrigation and modernization of farms.
    Lack of major industries, the majority of the working population being involved in the agricultural sector.
  2. Potential tourism
    Thanks to its history linked to Emir Abdelkader, the commune could develop historical tourism focused on:

The remains and stories of the capture of the Smala in 1843.
Agritourism, highlighting local traditions and the landscapes of the High Plateaux.


Conclusion
The economy of Z’malet El Émir Abdelkader remains largely agricultural and pastoral, with strong potential in sheep and cattle breeding. Local trade and crafts play a complementary role, while historical tourism could be a lever for future development.

Administration of Z’malet El Émir Abdelkader

  1. Administrative status
    Z’malet El Émir Abdelkader is a municipality in the wilaya of Tiaret, located in the northwest of Algeria. It is administered under the authority of the delegated Wilaya of Ksar Chellala, an administrative subdivision of the wilaya.
  • Wilaya: Tiaret (Code 14)
  • Delegated Wilaya: Ksar Chellala
  • Postal Code: 14038
  1. Institutions and local management
    The municipal administration is ensured by:
  • The Municipal Popular Assembly (APC): Body elected by the local population, responsible for the management of municipal affairs (urban planning, roads, health, education, etc.).
  • The mayor (President of the APC): He is elected for a 5-year term and represents the State at the local level.
  • The town hall (APC headquarters): Administrative center where official documents are issued (birth, marriage, death certificates, identity cards, etc.).
  1. Administrative services
    The municipality has several services to meet the needs of citizens:
  • Civil status: Registration of births, marriages and deaths.

  • Social affairs: Aid for poor families, pensions and allowances.
  • Education: Management of primary schools and support for school infrastructure.
  • Health: Presence of a polyclinic or a municipal health center.

4. Security and justice
– National Gendarmerie: Ensures security and public order in the municipality and its surroundings.
– Nearest court: Located in Ksar Chellala or Tiaret, for legal matters.

Development projects
The local administration is working on several areas of improvement:

  • Improving road infrastructure to facilitate access to rural areas.
  • Supporting agriculture and livestock with irrigation projects and aid to farmers.
  • Electrification and access to drinking water for some remote areas.
  • Education and health: Strengthening schools and medical facilities.


Conclusion
The administration of Z’malet El Émir Abdelkader plays a key role in the management of public services and local development. Although the commune is rural, the local authorities are striving to improve the quality of life of the inhabitants through various infrastructure and modernization projects.

]]> The historic figure of Emir Abdelkader /en/the-historic-figure-of-emir-abdelkader/ Sun, 09 Mar 2025 14:27:23 +0000 http://taguinezea.local/?p=102 Biography of Emir Abdelkader

Emir Abdelkader (1808-1883): Hero of the Algerian Resistance and Humanist

Emir Abdelkader is an emblematic figure in the history of Algeria and the Arab world. A military leader, statesman and thinker, he is recognized for his resistance to French colonization and for his humanist values.

Early Life and Education
Born in 1808 in the Mascara region (Algeria), Abdelkader belonged to a religious and scholarly family of the Qadiriyya Sufi brotherhood. From a young age, he received a thorough education in theology, philosophy and military sciences. He traveled to Mecca, Egypt and other Muslim countries, thus strengthening his knowledge and leadership.

Resistance against French Colonization (1832-1847)
Faced with the French invasion in 1830, the Algerian tribes sought a leader. In 1832, Abdelkader was proclaimed emir by several tribal chiefs in western Algeria. He organized the resistance and founded a state with a structured administration, a regular army and an effective diplomatic policy.

He won several victories against the French, thanks in particular to his knowledge of the terrain and his guerrilla tactics. However, faced with French military power and internal betrayals, he was forced to surrender in 1847 after the destruction of his bases by French forces.

Exile and Humanist Commitment
After his surrender, he was imprisoned in France (at the Château d’Amboise) until 1852. Freed by Napoleon III, he settled in Damascus (Syria), where he devoted himself to study and spirituality.

In 1860, he saved thousands of Christians during the intercommunal massacres in Damascus, which earned him international tributes, notably from France and the Pope.

Legacy and Death
Emir Abdelkader spent the last years of his life in Damascus, where he died in 1883. He is recognized as a hero of the Algerian resistance, an enlightened thinker and a humanist. In 1966, his remains were transferred to Algeria and rest in the El Alia cemetery.

His legacy lives on through his commitment to freedom, tolerance and justice.
